# 2 Evidence of relevance

Evidence status: direct stakeholder research has not yet been performed. The need for manuscript checks is supported indirectly by existing products [D01,D02] and research on scientific feedback [S01-S03,S12]. These sources motivate the problem but do not prove that our target users need the proposed workflow.

| Assumption to validate | How to collect evidence | Decision affected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authors miss rules while checking multiple files. | Ask 3-5 authors/supervisors to describe their most recent submission and a specific missed rule. | Prioritize upload, criteria confirmation and evidence navigation. |
| Authors need editable conference-specific criteria. | Ask users to bring an actual call for papers or submission checklist and identify ambiguous requirements. | Decide which criteria are structured and how ambiguity is resolved. |
| Users trust a finding more when they can inspect its source. | Show the prototype and ask users to locate the evidence for a sample result. | Set evidence display and explanation acceptance criteria. |
| External AI processing and runtime are acceptable. | Ask what manuscript material they may send externally, acceptable waiting time and cost constraints. | Define consent, supported deployment and cost limits. |

Collect 3-5 brief discussions with representatives who have actually prepared or supervised a scientific submission, or obtain substantive agreement from a real client. Record participant role, date, consent, concrete example, finding and requirement affected. Names and contact details need not enter the repository. Do not publish manuscripts or recordings without permission.

The separate interview kit contains questions, a blank evidence log and a synthesis template. A record becomes verified evidence only after an actual discussion. Planned participants, example answers and assumptions must not be counted as interviews.
