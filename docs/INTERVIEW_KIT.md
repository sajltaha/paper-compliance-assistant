# Short discussions with potential users

The assignment calls for 3–5 actual discussions with people who have written or checked scientific papers: students with submission experience, master's students, lecturers, authors or conference organizers. A substantive agreement with an actual client is an alternative. General approval of the topic alone does not establish that agreement.

Duration: 10–15 minutes. First explain the diploma project's purpose and ask permission to record anonymized notes. Audio recording is optional. Collect only necessary personal data. A written exchange with agreed anonymized excerpts is also suitable.

1. When did you last prepare or check a conference paper? What was your role?
2. Where were the requirements, and how did you compare the paper with them? Please give a specific example.
3. What was hardest to check? Was a manuscript returned because of requirements?
4. Which checks would you entrust to a system, and which would you keep under human control?
5. Does each finding need a link to a specific manuscript passage? Why?
6. Show prototype/index.html. Ask the participant to load the example, confirm the criteria and locate the evidence for one finding. What is unclear?
7. May manuscript text be sent to an external AI provider? What document and privacy restrictions apply?
8. What waiting time and cost per run would be acceptable?
9. Which 2–3 system requirements are essential to you?

For each actual discussion, record: participant ID such as U01; date; role and relevant experience; consent to notes; actual answers; a specific problem; a supporting quotation or anonymized record; implications for FR/NFR; and limitations. Use actual responses. After 3–5 discussions, group recurring problems and contradictions and update requirements.
